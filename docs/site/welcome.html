Plume is installed

Plume posts to your own website from anywhere in the browser. Nothing is sent anywhere except the site you connect it to.

What you need first

Plume publishes to your website, so your site has to be able to accept posts. Two open standards make that work, and you don't have to understand either one to use Plume:

  • Micropub is how Plume hands a post to your site — the equivalent of an app's "publish" button, but for a site you own.
  • IndieAuth is how you sign in. You log in with your domain name instead of an email and password, and Plume never sees a password — your site hands it a token it can revoke at any time.

Publishing platforms that support this include Micro.blog, Indiekit, and WordPress, Kirby and others via a plugin. The IndieWeb wiki keeps a fuller list of servers.

If your site doesn't support Micropub yet, Plume can't post to it. That's the one prerequisite — everything else below takes a couple of minutes.

Getting set up

  1. Pin Plume to your toolbar. Click the extensions icon in your browser's toolbar and pin the feather. You can also open the composer any time with Alt + Shift + P.
  2. Connect your site. Open Plume's settings and choose Add account, then enter your domain. Plume will show you each step as it goes: asking permission for your site, finding its endpoints, and sending you to your own server to approve the connection.
  3. Approve the connection on your own site. A tab opens on your server asking whether to let Plume post. That page belongs to you, not to Plume. Approve it and you'll land back here, connected.

What you can do

  • Write notes, articles, replies, bookmarks, likes, reposts and photo posts.
  • Right-click any page, link, selection or image to start a post about it — the URL and title are filled in for you.
  • Drafts save as you type, per site and per thing you're replying to, and are kept for seven days.
  • If a post fails because you're offline or your server is down, it's queued and retried in the background rather than lost.
  • Connect more than one site and switch between them from the composer's header.

If something goes wrong

Plume keeps a local debug log. Open its settings, scroll to Debug log, and press Copy — that text is what makes a bug report actionable. Access tokens and authorization codes are stripped out before anything is written to it, so it's safe to paste in public.

Report anything odd at github.com/rmdes/plume/issues.
